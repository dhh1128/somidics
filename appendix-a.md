# Appendix A — Annotated Bibliography for Somidion Prevalence Quantification  
*(Tiered, non-exhaustive; intended as an agenda-setting resource rather than evidentiary proof)*

This appendix enumerates **high-quality (Tier A) and acceptable secondary (Tier B)** sources that demonstrate the *feasibility* of quantifying somidion and anti-somidion prevalence.  
Numeric values are illustrative only; readers are directed to the cited works for definitions, cohorts, and methods.

---

## Tier A — Primary, population-based or clinically grounded sources

### Tattoos (presence, site, visibility)

**Laumann, A. E., & Derick, A. J. (2006).**  
*Tattoos and body piercings in the United States: A national data set.*  
Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, 55(3), 413–421.  
**What it measures:**  
- Tattoo prevalence in a U.S. national probability sample (ages 18–50).  
- Tattoo body regions (face, neck, arms, hands/fingers, trunk, legs, feet).  
- “Exposed” vs “covered” tattoos (operationalized by site).  
**Why relevant to somidics:**  
Provides one of the strongest existing empirical bases for **region-specific** and **visibility-conditioned** somidions, and for whole-body anti-somidions (“no tattoos anywhere”).

---

### Piercings (presence and site)

**Bone, A., Ncube, F., Nichols, T., & Noah, N. D. (2008).**  
*Body piercing in England: A survey of piercing at sites other than earlobe.*  
BMJ, 336, 1426–1428.  
**What it measures:**  
- Population prevalence of non-earlobe piercings in England (≥16).  
- Site-specific prevalence (nose, eyebrow, lip, tongue, nipple, navel, ear cartilage, genital).  
**Why relevant to somidics:**  
Unusually clean example of **whole-population, site-specific prevalence**, directly mappable to anatomical zones and suitable for both positive and negative predicates.

---

### Melanocytic nevi (moles)

**Jackson, A., & Black, S. (2003).**  
*Forensic examination of the dorsum of the hand.*  
Journal of Forensic Sciences.  
**What it measures:**  
- Presence of melanocytic nevi and other marks on the dorsum of the hand.  
**Why relevant to somidics:**  
Demonstrates that **highly specific anatomical zones** (e.g., back of hand) are empirically studied and quantifiable, supporting fine-grained somidions.

**Various dermatology epidemiology studies (e.g., British Journal of Dermatology; JAAD).**  
**What they measure:**  
- Total-body and regional melanocytic nevus prevalence under explicit clinical definitions (often ≥2 mm).  
- Strong age dependence (childhood/adolescence vs older adults).  
**Why relevant:**  
Shows that “no nevi anywhere” and “nevi in region X” are both measurable and cohort-dependent, validating both somidions and anti-somidions.

---

### Scars

**Amici, J.-M., et al. (2022).**  
*Prevalence and characteristics of scars in five countries.*  
Journal of the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology.  
**What it measures:**  
- Self-reported prevalence of scars across multiple countries.  
**Why relevant:**  
Demonstrates that even ubiquitous mark classes can be quantified at population scale, enabling bounded estimates for both presence and absence.

---

### Congenital anomalies / missing parts

**Goldfarb, C. A., et al. (2017).**  
*Upper limb congenital anomalies in New York State.*  
Journal of Pediatric Orthopaedics.  
**What it measures:**  
- Population-based registry data on congenital upper-limb anomalies.  
**Why relevant:**  
Provides **low-prevalence, high-stability** somidions suitable for high-entropy discrimination and long-term persistence.

---

## Tier B — Secondary but methodologically transparent sources

### Tattoos and piercings (international comparisons)

**Sagoe, D., Pallesen, S., & Andreassen, C. S. (2017).**  
*Prevalence and correlates of tattooing in Norway.*  
Scandinavian Journal of Psychology.  
**What it measures:**  
- Tattoo prevalence and visibility categories in a large national sample.  
**Why relevant:**  
Useful for **cross-cultural sensitivity analysis** and visibility-conditioned modeling, with clear caveats.

---

### Forensic tattoo motif catalogs (text vs pictorial)

**Holz, F., et al. (2022).**  
*A guide to classify tattoo motives in Mexico as a tool to identify unknown bodies.*  
International Journal of Legal Medicine.  
**What it measures:**  
- Tattoo motif categories (letters, numbers, symbols, images) in forensic databases.  
**Why relevant:**  
Supports the plausibility of **semantic somidions** (e.g., presence of text-based tattoos), even when tattoo-level denominators vary.

---

## Notes on excluded material

Sources relying on:
- popular health websites,  
- commercial aggregators (“most tattooed country” rankings), or  
- tertiary summaries without transparent methodology  

have been **excluded** from this appendix to avoid reviewer objections. Such material may still serve as *leads* for future sourcing but is not suitable for inclusion in an academic bibliography.

---

## How this appendix should be read

- The purpose is **not** to assert definitive prevalence values.  
- The purpose is to show that **multiple independent, mature literatures already measure candidate somidions** with sufficient rigor to justify a formal framework.  
- Future work can unify these sources under standardized definitions, anatomical zoning, and correlation modeling as outlined in Section X (Measurement Agenda).
